Log and exponential properties

Here, log is the natural logarithm and exp(u) = eu. The symbol × means multiplication. Let a, b > 0; u, v, and c may be any real numbers.

PropertyRuleExample / reminder
Undo a logarithmexp(log(a))=aexp(log(4))=4
Undo an exponentiallog(exp(u))=ulog(exp(2))=2
Log of a quotientlog(ab)=log(a)−log(b)log(82)=log(8)−log(2)
Log of a productlog(a×b)=log(a)+log(b)log(6)=log(2)+log(3)
Log of a powerlog(ac)=c×log(a)log(42)=2×log(4)
Add in the exponentexp(u+v)=exp(u)×exp(v)exp(log(3)+log(2))=6
Subtract in the exponentexp(u−v)=exp(u)exp(v)exp(log(8)−log(2))=4
Reference valueslog(1)=0
exp(0)=1		Odds of 1 correspond to log-odds of 0.
Signs and rangeexp(u)>0
log(a)<0 if 0<a<1		Exponentials are always positive. Logs of numbers below 1 are negative.

Remember: log(a + b) ≠ log(a) + log(b).
